Trabant
Leap Agent

Target system architecture

A blueprint for a mobility support service that cites approved policy, asks before acting, and hands sensitive decisions to people.

Cite the source

Answer from approved, applicable evidence. Abstain when the evidence is missing or conflicting.

Ask before acting

Let the agent propose. Let authorized services confirm, execute, and record a transaction.

Hand off safely

Give urgent requests priority and a clear human path, even when AI or a partner service is unavailable.

How to read claims

Implemented means present in the demo. Target means proposed. A gate requires evidence or sign-off before the relevant capability is opened.

What this proposal leaves open

Region and feature availability, model quota, partner contracts, licensing, retention, and service levels require validation. The document does not select a subscription or provision resources.

Starting points: Hack to Build concept, original reference diagram, and implemented demo architecture.

01 / Product and scope

What the target should deliver

Give owners, advisors, and service partners one reliable support entry point without replacing the systems that own policy, entitlement, and booking records.

Initial journeys

  • Warranty and maintenance: explain the applicable policy for a model, market, and effective date, with links to the exact approved sections. Separately check entitlement if the user is signed in and has access to the vehicle.
  • Workshop appointment: find eligible service partners, create a draft from live availability, and accept an explicit confirmation. Show a booking reference only after the workshop system confirms it.
  • Safety and uncertainty: stop the automated dialogue when the request needs a person. Offer a direct fallback when a queue is unavailable. Never present a guess as policy or diagnose a vehicle.

Non-goals and assumptions

No diagnosis, remote vehicle commands, purchases, payment handling, automatic warranty approval, or unrestricted access to customer records. The first controlled pilot assumes one vehicle model, two service partners, limited accounts, and a reviewed market and language set. Live data and write access expand only after the applicable gates are met.

Keep the demo as a sandbox. Retain it for synthetic walkthroughs, without wiring its resources or identity into production. Its test results prove the demo flows, not production capacity, safety, reliability, or business impact.

02 / Application boundaries

Logical architecture

The interaction gateway owns identity and workflow state. The model has neither transaction credentials nor permission to commit an action.

Who owns each outcome?

Interaction

The gateway returns a typed answer, draft, action status, or handoff. It owns the user-visible request and correlation ID.

Records

Content owners govern published policy. Warranty and booking providers own eligibility and acceptance. Dynamics owns support cases.

Execution

Functions enforce policy and persist workflow state. A human owns exceptions and decisions outside delegated policy.

The network topology describes how the channels reach this design. The data ownership matrix distinguishes a convenience copy from an authoritative record.

Channels and user journeys

One request, three possible paths

  1. Question: Accept the request with locale and scoped vehicle context. Verify that the current user can see the matching policy. Return the answer with tappable citation links and show why a model, market, or vehicle detail is needed when ambiguous.
  2. Action: Separate reading availability from committing a booking. Show workshop, time zone, time, affected vehicle reference, and any material conditions. A change or expiry requires a new preview and confirmation. The UI distinguishes pending, confirmed, unavailable, and needs-review states.
  3. Escalation: Interrupt for urgent, ambiguous safety, or sensitive requests. Offer a clear next step immediately, and display transfer status separately. Do not block the user on an AI turn or say a person is connected before acknowledgment.

Accessibility and localization

Design for keyboard, screen reader, and mobile use (target WCAG 2.2 AA). Announce status transitions without stealing focus; do not use color alone to signal unsafe or pending states. Preserve policy section and effective date in translations, test time-zone rendering, and fall back to a human if no approved localized safety copy is available.

Channel parity is a release gate. If a channel cannot securely authenticate the user or display meaningful confirmation, keep that journey read-only or send the user to the first-party web experience.

04 / Trust and access

Identity and authorization

A successful sign-in identifies the caller. It does not grant access to a vehicle, policy, dealer, or operation on its own.

Proposed identity boundaries [02, 03]
PrincipalAuthenticationAuthorization scope
OwnerMicrosoft Entra External ID external tenant for customer identities. First-party session and token validation.Only linked vehicles and the customer's own authorized operations. Verify transfers and changes to ownership in the system of record.
Employee / advisorWorkforce tenant, role assignment, and conditional access. Privileged roles require step-up and time-limited elevation.Queue, market, and case assignment. Sensitive lookups use purpose and relationship checks, not organization-wide read access.
Dealer / partnerEntra B2B in the workforce tenant for named partner users; periodic external access review.Only the partner's own workshop locations and assigned referrals. Offboard promptly when a relationship ends.

How a request is authorized

  1. Validate signature, issuer, audience, expiry, and required scopes for that surface. For a browser session, use secure, HTTP-only cookies, anti-CSRF controls, and origin checks. The identity directory and business account mapping are explicit; never link on email alone.
  2. Resolve a trusted actor and tenant context from verified identity plus current vehicle or dealer relationships. The request and model may suggest an opaque reference, but cannot assign a role, market, consent state, or tenant.
  3. At the private API boundary, validate the caller service token. Propagate the actor through a short-lived, audience-bound internal assertion with purpose and allowed operation. Use delegated OAuth only where the integration supports that tenant and grant. Never pass bearer tokens to prompts or tools.
  4. Recheck role, resource ownership, policy version, and any required step-up before confirming and before dispatching a queued write. A worker uses its own identity and the recorded intent, not a stored user access token. Reject if current authorization cannot be established.
Fail closed on identity ambiguity. Separate anonymous, customer, workforce, and partner cohorts. Test both denial and successful access for each cohort, including revoked membership and direct object access to citations, cases, and operation status.

05 / Applied AI

AI runtime and guardrails

Microsoft Foundry Agent Service and an Azure OpenAI model are proposed for interpreting requests and explaining evidence. They are not the policy engine or a transaction authority.

The bounded turn

  1. Apply input limits, privacy filtering, and approved safety rules in the gateway. Route urgent or uncertain safety requests to the deterministic path before retrieval or model invocation. A user can request a person at any time.
  2. Resolve business intent and eligible context. For a policy question, the gateway fetches authorized excerpts through the retrieval adapter. Send only the question and the minimum relevant context; omit credentials, raw identifiers, unrelated case text, and hidden policy metadata. Keep the agent on approved tools and sources; no open-web access in the pilot.
  3. Call a pinned Foundry agent/model deployment. If the agent proposes a read tool, the app checks its name, input schema, current actor and policy, executes it, and returns a minimal typed result [04]. Unknown tools or arguments are refused. Cap iterations, elapsed time, token count, and concurrent work per identity.
  4. Treat the model output as a proposal. Validate its outcome schema and source IDs; cross-check citations against the exact retrieved versions. A valid citation is necessary but not sufficient: verify that the cited passages support the material claims. When validation is inconclusive, quote the approved text without expanding it or abstain. Apply safety and disclosure policy before rendering.
Typed outcomes returned by the gateway
OutcomeTrust and display rule
Cited answer / clarifyShow checked source IDs, date, scope, and exact section link. When model, market, or date is unknown, ask rather than silently assume.
Draft proposed / handoff requiredRender a server-side draft or approved escalation guidance. No language-model tool may confirm an appointment or invent a queue receipt.
Abstain / action statusState that the evidence is insufficient, or display an existing server-verified transaction state. Use deterministic text for receipts.

Safety and evidence checks

Use Azure AI Content Safety and model content filters as supporting controls, not a substitute for business policy. Treat retrieved documents, user messages, partner responses, and tool outputs as untrusted data, never instructions. Keep confirmation, escalation, permissions, and release criteria outside the prompt. Track model version, prompt version, risk policy, and evaluation-set version per release. Never stream unchecked factual claims or an apparent confirmation.

Model selection is a release decision. Evaluate a compact and higher-capability Azure OpenAI model on the same cases, then choose the least expensive approved deployment that meets quality and latency goals. Check model, processing type, regional capacity, and safety controls together [05, 06]. Roll out changes to prompts, embeddings, and models as separate reviewed changes where possible.

06 / Retrieval-augmented generation

Knowledge and citations

The source library must know which version is valid, whom it applies to, and whether that audience can read it before any text reaches an AI model.

Ingest and publish

Source owners approve manuals, service bulletins, warranty policies, and safe support guidance before ingestion. Keep the canonical file or record under its owner's control. Reject unclassified content, extract text and section anchors, attach an owner and effective dates, and stage the new index before publishing. Use a versioned embedding deployment; change it by rebuilding and comparing the new index, not by mixing incompatible vectors. Approve language variants and retire withdrawn versions explicitly.

Retrieve and verify

Use distinct public and restricted corpora; split further where required by tenant or dealer exposure. For restricted content, enforce tenant, audience, market, model, locale, and effective-date scope in the retrieval service. Security filters must be derived from trusted roles or entitlement, never model-supplied. Use hybrid lexical and vector retrieval, optional semantic reranking, and vector prefiltering where supported [07, 08]. Abstain when evidence is missing or conflicts. An embedding or rank score is not an entitlement decision.

Minimum metadata for every citable excerpt
MetadataWhy it matters
Identity and revisionDocument ID, immutable version, excerpt/section ID, checksum, content owner, publication state.
ApplicabilityEffective and expiry dates, model/market/locale, classification, and audience or access tags.
Citation and removalStable reference to the excerpt via an authorization-checked route, plus index version and withdrawal timestamp.

Measure time from approval or revocation to query visibility in both regions. Recheck restricted link access when opened. On withdrawal or uncertain propagation, block the affected scope until refreshed; do not serve stale restricted answers from a shared cache.

07 / Action service

Confirming and executing actions

A user confirms a specific, versioned draft. A successful HTTP call or an AI reply does not count as a booking receipt.

  1. Preview: Query live availability using a scoped read identity. Create an opaque draft bound to actor, vehicle, workshop, slot/version, expiry, and material conditions. Return a review card; do not reserve or change a record unless a separate approved hold policy exists.
  2. Confirm: An explicit user event presents the draft ID and idempotency key. Recheck current eligibility, actor, terms, expiry, and slot version. In one SQL transaction, consume the draft, create the operation record, and write an outbox row. Duplicate key plus same request returns the recorded status; a different request with the same key is rejected.
  3. Dispatch: An outbox relay publishes to Service Bus. A worker consumes with peek-lock, rechecks the operation and bounds retries, then calls the partner with the stable operation ID. Persist provider acknowledgment and final state before marking the message complete. Consumers handle redelivery idempotently [11, 12].
  4. Reconcile: If the provider times out after a possible commit, look up that operation first. Never blindly repeat an ambiguous non-idempotent write. Poll or surface pending status; escalate to an advisor when the partner cannot verify it. The partner remains the source of truth.
Example API surface; route names are illustrative (outbox rationale in [22])
OperationContract
POST /v1/appointments/previewReturns draft, display terms, and expiry. No durable external booking.
POST /v1/appointments/confirmReturns 202 and operation ID on acceptance; 409 for stale or mismatched draft. The model has no access to this route.
GET /v1/operations/{id}Access-checked status; includes the provider's reference only after verified acceptance.

If a provider cannot meet the identifier or reconciliation contract, keep that provider's booking journey human-assisted. Start by adding read-only warranty, workshop, and status adapters; add create, cancel, and reschedule only under their own approval rules.

08 / Service operations

Human handoff and failure behavior

Make escalation an actual workflow with a receipt, an owner, and a fallback. Do not ask a person in danger to wait for the chatbot.

Escalation protocol

  1. Apply approved safety categories and locale-aware rules before AI. Present safety-team-approved wording and the appropriate local emergency or roadside route immediately for urgent concerns. Do not diagnose or generate repair instructions for a high-risk event.
  2. Build a minimized handoff package: reason code, urgency, locale, verified identity state, scoped vehicle and case references, approved citations already shown, and the last action state. Share only necessary context with an assigned queue; distinguish user-reported facts from AI summaries.
  3. Route incidents to the relevant Dynamics 365 Customer Service queue. Agree routing rules and staffed hours with operations. For native Copilot Studio handoff, require a supported channel, compatible licenses, and the same Power Platform environment as Customer Service [09]. Validate the first-party web path separately.
  4. Use offered, queued, and accepted as separate statuses. If the queue cannot acknowledge, surface an alternate human route, persist the case only if that workflow is available, and state that the transfer did not occur. Do not send the full transcript by default.

Service ownership: Safety owns approved wording and escalation thresholds; Customer Service owns routing and staffing; Platform owns technical availability. A human advisor may override policy only through an existing authorized, auditable business workflow.

09 / Platform design

Azure network and hosting topology

Expose a deliberate HTTPS edge for interactive channels. Keep AI, tools, and data private. Connections to managed SaaS are an explicit exception, not implicit trust.

Link approved private DNS zones and test resolution from each runtime and deployment runner. Route outbound traffic through approved egress; authenticate and restrict SaaS callbacks. Keep SaaS routes and deployment access documented separately from the private data plane.

A partner or SaaS service outside the VNet still needs scoped credentials, bounded egress, and a contract for callback identity and replay handling. Expose callbacks only at a separate authenticated gateway route; do not expose a worker or database endpoint to the public internet.

Data ownership, privacy, and governance

Minimize what a conversation needs to know. Preserve the source of truth and separate transaction evidence from debug traces.

Processing and retention

Privacy owners approve purpose and legal basis by journey. Distinguish consent to communicate or share case data from confirmation of an appointment. Set separate retention for session state, telemetry, cases, and audit. Define access and deletion across primary stores, search, provider state, queues, logs, and backups; track legal holds separately.

Residency, secrets, and oversight

Validate processing locations for edge traffic, SaaS, backups, AI inference, and manual review; resource location alone is not sufficient. Prefer private access, managed identity, TLS, and encryption at rest. Use RBAC, soft delete, and purge protection for Key Vault [17]. Catalog sensitive assets in Purview and assign ownership for connector policy and Defender alerts.

Production gate: sign off a data-flow inventory, residency constraints, retention schedule, and audit access model before admitting real data. Do not infer regulatory compliance from Azure service selection.

11 / Resilience

Reliability and disaster recovery

Degrade to useful read-only or human support. Keep writes fenced to one region until the new writer and provider reconciliation are verified.

Proposed engineering objectives; validate with measured pilot data
SignalDraft targetHow to measure
Core journey availability99.9% monthly for eligible authenticated requests.Count timely, contract-correct outcomes, including warranted abstention, not HTTP 200 alone. Dependency failure is not successful abstention. Report partner outcomes separately.
Policy answer latencyP95 at or below 5 seconds to a validated outcome.Per locale and journey under agreed pilot load; track first status signal separately from final answer.
Action acceptanceP95 at or below 2 seconds to queued status, not partner confirmation.Time explicit confirmation to durable commit. Report partner receipt time and aged pending separately.
Disaster recoveryRTO within 60 minutes; workflow data RPO within 5 minutes pending drills.Measure from the regional incident to verified restore and last recoverable record. Neither is a provider guarantee.

Normal operations and pressure

Run zone-redundant compute where supported and separate workers from interactive turns. Apply request deadlines, bounded queues, jittered retries for safe reads, and per-dependency circuit breakers. Reserve capacity for safety and recovery work. Scale and alert on p95 latency, concurrency, queue age, and model/search throttling rather than CPU alone. Use static approved support only when its scope and version are known.

Regional failover runbook

  1. Incident lead disables new writes, captures the last confirmed operation watermark, and verifies that the old writer is fenced. Serve safe read-only support or handoff from an approved recovery origin.
  2. Validate private DNS, identity, partner connectivity, approved knowledge version, and quota in the secondary region. Rebuild the index from approved sources rather than assuming a cross-region search replica. Keep a durable outbox so queue recovery does not depend on the broker alone.
  3. Promote the database using the approved SQL failover policy. Regional replication is asynchronous; forced failover may lose recent records [18]. Reconcile with partner receipts and replay the outbox only with deduplication and authorization checks.
  4. Run synthetic read, handoff, and write-verification probes. Reopen writes gradually after owner approval. Plan a failback drill, document evidence, and keep failback separate from recovery.

Evaluate Sweden Central as primary and a second compliant region for recovery. Confirm all paired dependencies, SaaS geographies, private links, and quotas together. Do not switch to a global model deployment as an unreviewed workaround for a regional outage.

12 / Run the service

Operational signals and response

Measure whether users received a correct and safe outcome, not only whether the model or endpoint answered.

Observability and audit

Propagate a server-generated correlation ID and W3C trace context across the gateway, retrieval, Functions, outbox, and queue processing. Send minimized OpenTelemetry metrics and traces to Azure Monitor/Application Insights with controlled Log Analytics retention [19]. Measure technical availability and safety independently. Audit records are not subject to normal trace sampling and do not contain secrets or prompt transcripts.

Minimum audit and release evidence

Record actor reference and role, policy and release version, action or escalation type, server decision, confirmation and receipt references where relevant, timestamp in UTC, and correlation ID. Logs and business records must distinguish request, approval, execution, and reconciliation. Restrict access and verify expiry; use immutable retention only where approved and compatible with deletion and legal-hold obligations.

Operational owner required. Every production alert needs a named team, threshold, response window, and runbook link. Before pilot, practice a stale-policy rollback, model outage, partner timeout, and handoff-queue failure with real on-call ownership.

13 / Engineering and release controls

Environments, delivery, and security checks

Ship infrastructure, code, content, policy, and AI configuration as coordinated but independently reversible releases.

Environment design

Separate development, staging, and production subscriptions or approved isolation boundaries; use distinct Power Platform environments, identities, DNS routes, resources, quotas, and secret scopes. Keep development synthetic. Build staging to validate production network, permissions, and recovery using test partners and nonproduction identities. Never reuse the demo's app registration or data store for production.

Repeatable release pipeline

  1. Manage Azure resources as modular Bicep. Use a separate approved workflow for Entra and Power Platform configuration. In CI use GitHub Actions OpenID Connect federation with environment-scoped roles, protected branches, and separate approvals for production [20]. Use private runner access where needed.
  2. Build immutable artifacts and record runtime, API, model, prompt, tool schema, policy, and knowledge index versions. Validate Bicep, scan artifacts and dependencies, and review the ARM what-if output before deployment. Keep data-plane permissions and connector owners in the release checklist.
  3. Run integration and policy evaluations in staging; use small, reversible canaries. Version API contracts and use expand-and-contract schema changes. Switch to a prior approved index or compatible app release on regression. Do not roll back a partner booking by replaying an old artifact.
  4. Use independent flags for generative answers, partner writes, and each channel. Safety handoff stays available when an AI feature is disabled. Keep a kill switch for each connector, test it in drills, and record privileged changes.

Operational readiness includes a maintained inventory, service owners, rotation and offboarding jobs, backup and restore exercises, and independent security and accessibility sign-off.

14 / Path to production

Rollout and acceptance criteria

Expand in stages. Passing a prototype walkthrough does not authorize live data access or writes.

Illustrative eight-week pilot sequence; progress depends on gates
WindowDeliverableExit gate

Quality and safety gates

  • Citations and groundedness: target at least 90% of factual answers with an approved, applicable citation and at least 85% advisor-rated grounded-answer acceptance. Sample all scoped market and language combinations; verify authorization and exact excerpts.
  • Completion: target at least 80% correct task completion across the three scoped journeys. Count an appropriate handoff as success only when the expected next step is delivered. Track successful partner receipt separately.
  • Non-negotiable: no observed critical privacy or safety failure or unintended booking in the controlled release set. Required escalation paths pass every defined case. A failed or underpowered safety evaluation blocks expansion; zero observed failures do not establish zero risk.

Build evaluation sets with content owners and advisors. Include sparse or conflicting policy, multilingual and inaccessible sources, entitlement denial, partner errors, and recovery drills. Use model-based evaluation to triage, with human review for safety, disagreement, and sampled output quality [06].

Measure value against an agreed baseline. The concept's 25% handling-time reduction, 35% informational containment, and 30% reduction in incomplete handoffs are hypotheses, not achieved results. Publish denominators and confidence limits where meaningful.

15 / Decisions and trade-offs

Sizing, costs, and open decisions

Prove demand and correctness before committing to multi-region capacity or widening the set of permitted actions.

Capacity and cost model

Measure peak requests per second, concurrent conversations, turns per journey, input and output tokens per turn, indexed chunk count, query bursts, partner rate limits, and daily run hours. Estimate model spend by multiplying input and output tokens by their own unit prices; include embedding and evaluation calls. Estimate search, APIM, compute, SQL, messaging, backup, edge, monitoring, and licenses separately. Include warm recovery capacity, egress, and on-call ownership.

Start with a quota-verified pay-as-you-go model and grow only after measuring concurrency and latency. Bound per-turn tokens, tool calls, trace volume, and transaction queue depth. Budget alerts are signals, not spending caps. Track periodic cost per completed eligible journey and per human handoff instead of just the monthly bill. No contract pricing or live resource configuration is assumed here.
